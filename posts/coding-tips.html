5 Habits That Made Me a Better Coder

Becoming a better developer isn't about memorizing every syntax detail. It's about building habits that compound over time. These five practices changed how I write code and how quickly I learn new things.

1. Read code before you write it

Before adding a new feature, I spend time understanding the existing codebase. It saves hours of rework and helps me match the project's style and patterns.

2. Write small, focused commits

Each commit should do one thing well. When something breaks, I can pinpoint the cause in minutes instead of hours.

3. Explain problems out loud

Rubber duck debugging works. Describing a bug to someone — or even to yourself — often reveals the solution before you finish the sentence.

4. Build side projects

Tutorials teach syntax. Projects teach judgment. Every small app I build teaches me something no course ever could.

5. Take breaks seriously

Staring at a screen for six straight hours rarely produces good code. A short walk or a cup of tea often unlocks the answer faster than another hour of grinding.
